Most builders have the opposite problem from the one they think they have. They believe they need to build more. They actually need to sell what they already built. I had 43 live products and 3 registers. This is the exact system I used to fix that, and it runs on Claude agents doing the boring parts.

01The one idea

Every thing you ship is a door. A visitor who walks through it should always be able to take one next step: pay you, or follow you. No dead ends. If a product cannot be paid for and does not point anywhere, it is a party with no host and no exit.

So the whole game is two moves, repeated across everything you own: put a register on it, and route every visitor somewhere.

02The doctrine: gifts and offers, never blended

Two clean modes, and you never mix them.

  • Gifts are free with zero strings. No email wall, no "free but". A whole gift.
  • Offers state the price in the first breath. The number is part of the dish, so collecting money is never an awkward ask, because the deal was priced before word twenty.

The line I keep on every storefront: every price is the whole price, every gift is a whole gift. Blending the two (a "free" thing that turns into a pitch) is what makes people distrust builders. Keep them clean and people relax, and relaxed people buy.

03The gravity well

You cannot save souls in an empty church. A perfect product with no visitors makes nothing. So you build a well that pulls people in and keeps them:

  • Gifts are the bait. A free tool, a calculator, a scan. It gives real value and asks nothing.
  • Education is the smell from the kitchen. Publish the truth of your craft (real prices, real teardowns, what actually works). Trust compounds.
  • Offers are the meal. Priced upfront, market rate, for a real problem.

Free pulls, education keeps, offers convert. The same crowd flows gift to lesson to purchase.

04The two rails

Every product gets exactly one of these, so nothing is a dead end:

  • Capital rail: a Stripe payment link. For anything that solves a problem for money.
  • Social capital rail: a link to your personal hub (for me, adampang.com). For content, community, and free tools. Social capital compounds into an audience that buys later.

A tool can be both: free to use (the bait) with a founding license for the fans who want to lock lifetime access. That is not blending a gift and an offer, it is a gift with a clearly separate, clearly priced door for believers.

05Mint the register

Pricing is a market, not a guess. Price each thing the way you would price a trading card: look at what the comparable sells for, then set yours. An AI email tool sits near Superhuman's thirty a month, so its lifetime founding license lands near seventy nine. A data dashboard sits near the twenty nine dollar indie tier. Anchor every price to a real comparable and you can defend it out loud.

Then mint it in one motion. I use a /mint command that creates the Stripe product, price, and payment link from a plain-English description. Fifteen products got a live register in one sitting this way. The tool does the clicking, you make the pricing call.

06Wire the register

A payment link nobody can see earns nothing. Put a small, tasteful call to action on each product's own page: "Founding license, twenty nine dollars" linked to its checkout, or "more from me" linked to the menu. One line in the hero or the footer. Now every product is its own funnel: the free thing pulls the crowd, the button converts the believers, the menu shows them everything else.

07The lead engine

Before customers, you need relevant visitors. The engine that finds them:

  1. Find with a semantic search (I use Exa): describe your ideal customer in a sentence and get real companies, people, and the rooms they gather in.
  2. Enrich with a data tool (I use Apollo): turn names into verified emails and a clean list.
  3. Invite with a sequence that leads with the gift, never the ask.
  4. Seat them at a booking link (cal.com) or an on-site checkout.

08The watering holes

Every dish attracts a different crowd, so there is no single "get traffic" move. You go to the exact room where a crowd already drinks and bring the one dish they came for. A finance calculator goes to the FIRE forums. A relocation map goes to the nomad forums. A maker tool goes to the maker subreddits and the tool directories. Map each product to its crowd and its room, and only spend effort on the crowds that can be walked to a register.

09The safe send-arm

Cold email is the always-on workhorse: legal for business, cheap, scalable. Send it from a separate warmed domain, never your main one, through a tool that allows cold outreach, and never send identical message bodies, which is the universal spam trigger on every channel. Keep messaging apps for people who already replied. This one paragraph will save you a banned account.

10Build in public

The transparency is the marketing. Pick one product, narrate the real numbers as they happen, and people buy into the story before they buy the thing. The single highest-leverage version: sell the meta. A cheap guide about how you do the work (like this one), a pack of your reusable agent skills, and a public revenue counter. Sell shovels during the gold rush.

11The skills pack

The boring parts run on a small set of Claude skills. The ones that carry the most weight:

  • /mint — turn a plain description into a live Stripe link.
  • /cofounder — diagnose the whole business and name the one constraint.
  • /perfect — take a department and drive it to real, tested deliverables.
  • /easy and /asap — cut a stuck task to its clear goal and move at safe speed.

A skill is just a markdown file that tells the agent how to do one job well. They are the most valuable files you own right now. Steal these, rename them, make them yours.

12Your first week

  1. Pick your five best products. Price each against a real comparable. Mint five registers.
  2. Put one buy button and one "more from me" link on each of those five sites.
  3. Ship one free gift with real value. Post it in the one room its crowd actually drinks in.
  4. Write the truth of your craft once, publicly. Let it pull.
  5. Start the meta product: the guide, the skills pack, the public counter.

That is the whole system. Not more building. A register on everything, a route for everyone, and the boring parts handed to the agents.
